## The question

Does a conflict change how the next trial is handled?

**What it is about.** Conflict adaptation. The idea: after an incongruent
trial, control is tightened, so the word gets through less on the next trial.
**Prediction:** the congruency effect is smaller after an incongruent trial
than after a congruent one. This is the congruency sequence effect.
**The alternative to rule out:** nothing is adjusted; trials that repeat a
colour or a word from the trial before are simply faster, and such repeats are
spread unevenly over the four combinations.

## The analysis

The congruency effect after a congruent trial against the congruency effect
after an incongruent trial.

## Ask your assistant

> Now I want to test whether the congruency effect depends on the trial before:
> is it smaller right after an incongruent trial? What is the hypothesis behind
> that, and what does the design need?

## What to check

- **Is the trial before a factor?** Count the four combinations of this trial
  and the trial before. Are they equal?
- **The first trial.** It has no trial before. What did the design do with it?
- **What else repeats?** Count how often the ink colour, and so the response,
  repeats in each of the four combinations. If it differs, the alternative
  explanation is still open. Did the assistant raise it?
- **What would removing all repeats cost?** Ask. With three colours and half
  of the trials congruent, something has to give.

## The idea

What happened on the trial before can be a factor of the design like any
other: a transition factor. Then it can be crossed with the rest. Whatever is
not in the crossing is left to chance, and it is worth counting.
