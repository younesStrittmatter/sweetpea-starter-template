## The question

Do people adjust to how frequent conflict is?

**What it is about.** Sustained control. The idea: when most trials are
incongruent, people keep control high throughout; when most are congruent,
they relax it. **Prediction:** the congruency effect is smaller in mostly
incongruent blocks than in mostly congruent blocks.
**The alternative to rule out:** in a mostly congruent block the word predicts
the ink, so people can simply learn to follow the word. That also makes
congruent trials fast and incongruent trials slow, without any change in
control.

## The analysis

The congruency effect in mostly congruent blocks against the congruency effect
in mostly incongruent blocks.

## Ask your assistant

> Now I want to test whether people adapt to the context. I want blocks in
> which most trials are congruent and blocks in which most are incongruent,
> and compare the congruency effect between them. What is the hypothesis, and
> what should I watch out for?

## What to check

- **The proportions.** Are they what you asked for, in every block?
- **How well does the word tell the ink?** Count, for each word, how often it
  appears in its own colour, in each kind of block. That number is the
  alternative explanation.
- **The thin cells.** How many incongruent trials does a mostly congruent block
  have? Is that enough?
- **What are the options?** Ask the assistant how the two explanations could be
  told apart, and what each way costs.

## The idea

An imbalance can be the manipulation: here it is a weight on a level. It still
has side effects, and the design should say which ones it accepts.
