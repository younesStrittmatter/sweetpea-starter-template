# Tasks and the questions they have been used for

An overview for suggesting a task when a user names a research question, and for seeing what a
design of that kind usually compares. One entry per published experiment with open data; the list
is a sample, not a survey. Generated by `dev/build_task_overview.py`; do not edit by hand.

How to use it: find the question or a neighbouring one, read what was compared and which factors
the design had, and offer the user one or two tasks with what each is good for. A factor listed
under "from the trial before" depends on the previous trial; the design has to say what happens to it.

## Action induced blindness

- **Question:** action and perception. **Task:** Prepare a left or right keypress to an arrow cue, identify a briefly shown, masked arrow while the response is prepared, execute the prepared response and then report the direction of the masked arrow.
  **Compared:** Compatibility of prepared response and masked target (identification performance). **Factors:** cue direction, target direction; derived: compatibility; from the trial before: previous compatibility, cue repetition, target repetition, target equals previous cue.
  Janczyk et al. (2023), Psychological Research, Experiment 2. doi:10.1007/s00426-022-01705-8

## Additional singleton search

- **Question:** statistical learning of distractor location. **Task:** Report the orientation of the line inside the shape singleton of an eight-item display; on two thirds of the trials one other item is a colour singleton distractor, which appears most often at one location, while the target appears equally often at every location.
  **Compared:** main effect of distractor position on response time. **Factors:** high probability location, target location, distractor location; derived: distractor condition, target position, distance; from the trial before: none.
  Failing et al. (2019), Attention, Perception, & Psychophysics, Experiment 2. doi:10.3758/s13414-019-01704-9

- **Question:** statistical learning of distractor location. **Task:** Additional singleton search: the orientation of the line inside the unique shape among eight is reported; on two thirds of the trials one other item has a unique colour, in the learning phase mostly at one location.
  **Compared:** learning phase, response time: distractor condition. **Factors:** phase, target location, distractor location, target shape, display colour, required response; derived: distractor condition, target at high probability location; from the trial before: previous distractor condition, target location repetition, target shape repetition, display colour repetition, response repetition, distractor location repetition.
  Golan et al. (2024), Journal of Cognition, Experiment 1. doi:10.5334/joc.382

- **Question:** statistical learning of distractor location. **Task:** Additional singleton search: the orientation of the line inside the unique shape among eight is reported; on two thirds of the trials one other item has a unique colour, in the learning phase mostly at one location; in the learning phase one group is cued to the location of the target.
  **Compared:** test phase, response time: statistical learning by group. **Factors:** group, phase, target location, distractor location, target shape, display colour, required response; derived: distractor condition, target at high probability location; from the trial before: previous distractor condition, target location repetition, target shape repetition, display colour repetition, response repetition, distractor location repetition.
  Golan et al. (2024), Journal of Cognition, Experiment 2. doi:10.5334/joc.382

- **Question:** statistical learning of distractor location. **Task:** Report the orientation of the line inside the diamond among circles of an eight-item display; on five sixths of the trials one circle is larger (a distractor of low or high saliency), most often at one location, and one of the two saliency levels is the more frequent one for a group of participants.
  **Compared:** LS group: low-saliency distractor at the high-probability location against no distractor. **Factors:** group, high probability location, target location, distractor location, distractor saliency, target line; derived: distractor position, target position; from the trial before: none.
  Gong et al. (2021), Attention, Perception, & Psychophysics, Experiment 3. doi:10.3758/s13414-020-02142-8

- **Question:** statistical learning of distractor location. **Task:** Additional singleton search in the MRI scanner: report whether the bar of unique orientation holds a dot; a colour singleton distractor appears more often at one of four locations; on a quarter of the trials no search display follows the placeholder.
  **Compared:** Main effect of distractor location on response time. **Factors:** trial kind, target location, distractor location, target colour, target orientation; derived: distractor position, target position; from the trial before: previous distractor position, target location repetition, target colour repetition, target orientation repetition.
  Richter et al. (2025), eLife, Single experiment. doi:10.7554/elife.101733

- **Question:** statistical learning of distractor location. **Task:** Report whether a line of the assigned orientation is present inside the shape singleton of a briefly shown, masked eight-item display; a colour singleton distractor, when present, appears most often at one location.
  **Compared:** sensitivity with the distractor at the high- against a low-probability location. **Factors:** high probability location, target location, distractor location, search orientation, target line; derived: signal, distractor condition, target position, distance; from the trial before: none.
  van Moorselaar et al. (2021), Journal of Vision, Single experiment. doi:10.1167/jov.21.12.3

- **Question:** statistical learning of distractor location. **Task:** Report whether a display of eight circles or diamonds holds one item of the other shape, while on half of the trials one item has another colour, far more often at one location than at the seven others.
  **Compared:** RT: target presence, displays without distractor or with it at a low-probability location. **Factors:** target presence, distractor presence, high probability location, target location, distractor location, display colour, display shape; derived: distractor condition, target position; from the trial before: previous target presence, previous distractor presence, previous distractor condition, previous target position, target presence repetition, distractor presence repetition, display colour repetition, display shape repetition, target location repetition, distractor location repetition, target at previous distractor location, distractor at previous target location.
  van Moorselaar et al. (2022), Attention, Perception, & Psychophysics, the experiment of the article. doi:10.3758/s13414-021-02330-0

- **Question:** statistical learning of distractor location. **Task:** Find the item with a unique shape among eight items on a circle and report the orientation of the line inside it; on two thirds of the trials one other item has a unique colour, most often at one location.
  **Compared:** distractor at a low-probability location minus at the high-probability location. **Factors:** high probability location, target location, distractor location, target line, display type; derived: distractor condition, target position; from the trial before: previous distractor condition, target location repetition, distractor location repetition, target line repetition, display type repetition.
  Vicente-Conesa et al. (2023), Attention, Perception, & Psychophysics, Experiment 2. doi:10.3758/s13414-022-02608-x

## Attention network test

- **Question:** anxiety and attention. **Task:** Attention Network Test (four cue types, flanker congruency) in alternating safe and threat-of-shock blocks.
  **Compared:** State x Cue (orienting network). **Factors:** state, cue, congruency, special; derived: none; from the trial before: previous congruency.
  Huang et al. (2023), Cognitive, Affective, & Behavioral Neuroscience, Single experiment. doi:10.3758/s13415-023-01133-0

## Attention shift cueing

- **Question:** attention shifting. **Task:** Rapid serial visual presentation at two locations: a letter cue signals to hold attention at the current stream or shift it to the other; then the parity of a digit is reported. Shift cues are frequent or rare per block, and each cue letter is frequent or rare within its type.
  **Compared:** response time: cue type by shift likelihood by stimulus identity likelihood. **Factors:** cue type, shift likelihood, stimulus identity likelihood, cue letter; derived: none; from the trial before: previous cue type, previous stimulus identity likelihood, cue letter repetition two back.
  Sali et al. (2025), Attention, Perception, & Psychophysics, Experiment 2. doi:10.3758/s13414-025-03137-z

## Breaking continuous flash suppression

- **Question:** statistical learning. **Task:** Report the orientation of a triangle that is shown to one eye and gradually breaks through a flickering mask shown to the other eye; the triangle appears on one side of fixation on three quarters of the trials.
  **Compared:** response time: low-probability minus high-probability side. **Factors:** probability, epoch, target orientation, mask eye; derived: none; from the trial before: preceding probability.
  Xu et al. (2024), Psychological Science, Experiment 1. doi:10.1177/09567976241263344

- **Question:** statistical learning. **Task:** Report without time pressure whether a triangle, shown to one eye for a duration set by a staircase while a flickering mask is shown to the other eye, was left or right of fixation; the triangle points in one direction on three quarters of the trials.
  **Compared:** localization accuracy: high-probability minus low-probability orientation. **Factors:** probability, target location; derived: none; from the trial before: previous probability, target location repetition.
  Xu et al. (2024), Psychological Science, Experiment 3. doi:10.1177/09567976241263344

## Change detection

- **Question:** scene memory. **Task:** A photograph of an outdoor scene is shown for 250 ms and, after a mask, a closer or a wider view of it; the participant reports by key press whether the second view is closer or farther.
  **Compared:** accuracy: advantage of wide-to-close over close-to-wide changes, large minus small aperture. **Factors:** view change, aperture, focus; derived: none; from the trial before: view change repetition, aperture repetition, focus repetition.
  Gandolfo et al. (2023), Psychological Science, Experiment 2. doi:10.1177/09567976221140341

- **Question:** scene memory. **Task:** A photograph of an outdoor scene, matched with the other photographs of the scene for low-level image properties, is shown for 250 ms and, after a mask, a closer or a wider view of it; the participant reports by key press whether the second view is closer or farther.
  **Compared:** accuracy: advantage of wide-to-close over close-to-wide changes, large minus small aperture. **Factors:** view change, aperture, focus; derived: none; from the trial before: previous view change, previous aperture, previous focus.
  Gandolfo et al. (2023), Psychological Science, Experiment 3. doi:10.1177/09567976221140341

## Change detection with search task

- **Question:** visual working memory. **Task:** Remember four coloured squares, report the gap orientation of a Landolt square among three items shown during the retention interval (or wait, on control trials), then judge whether one square of the memory display changed colour.
  **Compared:** control trials minus varied targets. **Factors:** block task, trial type, memory change; derived: task condition; from the trial before: previous trial type, memory change repetition.
  Hamblin-Frohman et al. (2023), Journal of Vision, Experiment 2. doi:10.1167/jov.23.7.15

## Choice reaction with memory updating

- **Question:** working memory updating. **Task:** Press a key for the letter X or O on every trial and keep in memory the last letter that appeared in a red frame, for recall at the end of a sequence of trials.
  **Compared:** response time: main effect of updating. **Factors:** updating, stimulus; derived: none; from the trial before: update switch, stimulus repetition.
  Kessler et al. (2023), Journal of Cognition, Experiment 2. doi:10.5334/joc.257

## Colour singleton search

- **Question:** priming of pop out. **Task:** Colour singleton search: find the diamond of the odd colour among three and report the side of its cut-off corner; the target colour follows a predictable pattern of pairs in two blocks (with and without saying the next colour aloud) and is unpredictable in a third.
  **Compared:** predictability (active, passive, baseline) by colour repetition. **Factors:** predictability, target colour, cut off side; derived: none; from the trial before: colour repetition, previous colour repetition.
  Cochrane et al. (2020), Attention, Perception, & Psychophysics, Experiment 2. doi:10.3758/s13414-020-02034-x

## Colour word contingency learning

- **Question:** contingency learning. **Task:** Report the print colour (purple, orange or grey) of a word with one of three keys; every word appears most often in one colour.
  **Compared:** counterconditioning: old against new high contingency. **Factors:** phase, sub block, word, colour, word set; derived: contingency; from the trial before: previous sub block, previous word set, previous contingency, word repetition, colour repetition, exact repetition.
  Schmidt et al. (2020), Collabra: Psychology, Experiment 2. doi:10.1525/collabra.320

## Cross modal matching

- **Question:** numerosity perception. **Task:** Feel an array of dowels and choose which of two visual dot arrays has the same number.
  **Compared:** main effect of pattern. **Factors:** pattern, ratio, magnitude, hull relation, correct side; derived: none; from the trial before: pattern repetition.
  Ziegler et al. (2023), Attention, Perception, & Psychophysics, Single experiment. doi:10.3758/s13414-023-02658-9

## Cued task switching

- **Question:** task switching. **Task:** Press the key of the goal box that a task cue names (the next box clockwise, the next box counter-clockwise, or the box across) from the starting box of a grid of four boxes, on a background pattern that repeats or changes from trial to trial.
  **Compared:** response time: task-switch cost on context repetitions minus context changes. **Factors:** task, context, start box, goal box; derived: none; from the trial before: task transition, context transition, previous task transition, previous context transition.
  Schiltenwolf et al. (2024), Psychonomic Bulletin & Review, Experiment 2. doi:10.3758/s13423-024-02465-9

- **Question:** task switching after preparation. **Task:** Classify a coloured shape by its colour or by its shape, as a cue word says, with the left or right index finger; on some trials only the cue is shown and no target follows.
  **Compared:** RT switch cost after a completed trial, cue-cue interval controlled. **Factors:** task, cue, preparation interval, trial completion, target colour, target shape, required response; derived: none; from the trial before: transition, preceding trial completion, preceding preparation interval, preceding trial cci controlled, previous transition, trial completion two back, preparation interval two back.
  Swainson et al. (2021), Psychological Research, Experiment 1. doi:10.1007/s00426-019-01254-7

## Distractor response binding

- **Question:** stimulus response binding. **Task:** Prime-probe letter classification with two letters per key; a task-irrelevant colour is shown as a box around the target (figure) or as the whole background, separately for prime and probe, and repeats or changes from prime to probe.
  **Compared:** Response relation x colour relation (distractor-response binding). **Factors:** prime target, probe target, prime colour, probe colour, prime layer, probe layer, prime response, probe response; derived: response relation, colour relation, target repetition; from the trial before: previous response relation, previous colour relation, previous prime layer, previous probe layer, prime target repetition, probe target repetition, prime colour repetition, probe colour repetition, prime response repetition, probe response repetition, prime layer repetition.
  Schmalbrock et al. (2022), Attention, Perception, & Psychophysics, Experiment 3. doi:10.3758/s13414-022-02511-5

## Episodic flanker task

- **Question:** attention and memory. **Task:** Memory list of four, five or six words shown vertically, then a probe word at a cued position: decide whether the probe word appeared at that position; lures come from one or two positions away.
  **Compared:** Distance effect by set size (linear) in error rate. **Factors:** set size, lag, cue position; derived: probe item position, probe item at list end, required response; from the trial before: previous set size, previous lag, cue position repetition, probe item position repetition, required response repetition.
  Logan et al. (2024), Attention, Perception, & Psychophysics, Experiment 9. doi:10.3758/s13414-024-02965-9

## Flanker task

- **Question:** object based attention. **Task:** Report the orientation of a briefly shown letter T in the ring nearer to fixation; a second T in the neighbouring ring is absent, of the same or of another orientation; the two rings are connected by a line (one object) or not.
  **Compared:** accuracy: grouping by distractor type. **Factors:** grouping, object location, target orientation; derived: distractor type; from the trial before: preceding distractor type.
  Bidiwala et al. (2021), Attention, Perception, & Psychophysics, Experiment 1. doi:10.3758/s13414-021-02325-x

- **Question:** cognitive control. **Task:** Report the colour (red or green) of the central square of a row of five squares; the four outer squares have one colour, and the word Red, Green or Cross precedes the row.
  **Compared:** interaction of prime compatibility (neutral, incompatible) and flanker congruency on response times. **Factors:** prime word, flanker colour, target colour; derived: prime compatibility, flanker congruency; from the trial before: previous prime compatibility, previous flanker congruency, target colour repetition.
  Dunaway et al. (2026), Psychological Research, Experiment 1. doi:10.1007/s00426-025-02216-y

- **Question:** ego depletion. **Task:** Indicate by key press the direction of the central arrow of five arrows whose outer four point the same or the opposite way, directly after an easy or a hard version of a colour Stroop task.
  **Compared:** error rate: main effect of the preceding Stroop type. **Factors:** preceding stroop type, flanker congruency, target direction; derived: none; from the trial before: previous congruency, target repetition.
  Radtke et al. (2025), Collabra: Psychology, Single experiment. doi:10.1525/collabra.151266

- **Question:** conflict processing. **Task:** Eriksen flanker task with two stimulus sets in separate blocks: a central letter (H or K) or a central arrow (left or right) is classified with a left or right key; the flanking images are congruent, incongruent or neutral (S, or a double-headed arrow).
  **Compared:** percentage correct: congruency. **Factors:** stimuli type, required response, target, flanker; derived: congruency; from the trial before: previous congruency, response repetition, flanker repetition.
  Smith et al. (2024), Quarterly Journal of Experimental Psychology, Experiment 1. doi:10.1177/17470218231201476

- **Question:** selective attention. **Task:** Press one of two keys for a visual target letter (H, S, Q or V, two letters per key) while a spoken letter, which starts half a second earlier, is to be ignored.
  **Compared:** congruency condition on response time. **Factors:** target letter, flanker letter, response; derived: congruency; from the trial before: previous congruency, target repetition, flanker repetition, response repetition.
  Ulrich et al. (2021), Attention, Perception, & Psychophysics, Experiment 2. doi:10.3758/s13414-020-02150-8

- **Question:** spatial selection under location uncertainty. **Task:** Report the direction of a central target arrowhead surrounded by twelve congruent or incongruent flanker arrowheads; the array appears at fixation in Fixed blocks and at one of four nearby positions in Variable blocks.
  **Compared:** interaction of location and congruency on the error rate. **Factors:** block type, position, target, flanker, inter trial interval; derived: congruency; from the trial before: location, previous location, previous congruency, target repetition, flanker repetition, inter trial interval repetition.
  Villalonga et al. (2025), Attention, Perception, & Psychophysics, Experiment 3. doi:10.3758/s13414-025-03026-5

## Free choice task

- **Question:** action control costs in task selection. **Task:** Choose freely one of two wagons, one moving with and one against the mouse, then swipe the mouse left or right as a letter cue says.
  **Compared:** compatible choices: effect of block. **Factors:** block number, cue direction; derived: none; from the trial before: previous cue direction.

## Majority judgement

- **Question:** ensemble perception. **Task:** Twelve faces with neutral and either happy or angry expressions are shown for 500 ms; the participant reports by key press whether neutral or emotional faces were more frequent.
  **Compared:** positive responses: angry minus happy session. **Factors:** facial expression, proportion; derived: majority; from the trial before: previous majority, proportion repetition.
  Ueda (2022), Attention, Perception, & Psychophysics, Experiment 1. doi:10.3758/s13414-022-02449-8

## Masked priming

- **Question:** masked number priming. **Task:** Indirect task: classify a target number (a digit or a German number word) as smaller or larger than five; a masked prime number precedes it, for a duration that differs between eight groups of participants.
  **Compared:** priming effect on response time in the group with a prime duration of 40 ms. **Factors:** prime duration, prime number, prime notation, target number, target notation; derived: congruency, number identity; from the trial before: none.
  Zerweck et al. (2021), Attention, Perception, & Psychophysics, Experiment 3. doi:10.3758/s13414-021-02312-2

## Old new recognition

- **Question:** recognition memory. **Task:** Visual search for a target letter in scene images, then a recognition confidence rating of old and new images, each followed by a search, then a second recognition confidence rating of the same old and new images.
  **Compared:** final recognition rating: old minus new images rated 1 in the familiarity judgment stage. **Factors:** stage, scene type, image category; derived: none; from the trial before: previous scene type, image category repetition.
  Lee et al. (2023), Cognition, Experiment 2. doi:10.1016/j.cognition.2023.105539

## Paired associate learning

- **Question:** memory interference between similar faces. **Task:** Learn twelve pairs of a profession word and a synthetic face, eight of the faces in four pairs of very similar faces; in the associative memory test a face is shown and its word is chosen among six words.
  **Compared:** accuracy of the associative memory test: condition, over the first nine rounds. **Factors:** condition, learning round, test group, face, base face, pairmate side, correct option position, competitor option position; derived: none; from the trial before: previous condition, correct option position repetition.
  Drascher et al. (2022), Psychonomic Bulletin & Review, Experiment 1. doi:10.3758/s13423-022-02082-4

## Parity judgement

- **Question:** contingency learning. **Task:** Classify a digit as odd or even after a display of letters, one or two of which predict the response on 90 percent of the learning trials.
  **Compared:** response time: cue saliency x validity. **Factors:** display, cue saliency, validity, cue prediction; derived: target parity; from the trial before: response repetition, cue prediction repetition, previous validity.

## Spatial cueing

- **Question:** priming of pop out. **Task:** Report on which side the small square lies inside the disk of unique colour among four disks (red among green or green among red); a red or green cue precedes the display at the position of the target or of another disk.
  **Compared:** interaction of cue validity and target-cue colour match on response times. **Factors:** target colour, cue colour, cue position, target position, target dot side, cued item dot side; derived: cue validity, cue target match, cued item compatibility; from the trial before: target cue match, target colour repetition, response repetition.
  Kerzel et al. (2025), Attention, Perception, & Psychophysics, Experiment 1. doi:10.3758/s13414-024-02998-0
